# Turn LANTERN’s failures into defensible research

**LANTERN is an early applied-research testbed with substantial engineering and useful negative results, but it is not ready to support its broad claim of reliable lunar sensor integrity or improved landing safety.** Its newer Unity pipeline genuinely connects estimated navigation, terrain matching, correction selection and control. However, its frozen transfer test achieved exactly the same successful cases as geometric matching while increasing speed-limit violations from **39/162 to 47/162**. The strongest next contribution is a focused study of when position-improving terrain corrections harm contact outcomes, and whether control-aware correction scheduling fixes that tradeoff. Before another headline experiment, repair measurement/time bookkeeping, verify the relevant physics, establish fair controls, and freeze a genuinely new terrain holdout. NASA relevance is real; generic lidar-to-map navigation is established prior work, and neither relevance nor simulation volume establishes novelty or acceptance. A credible simulation paper is achievable without flight qualification, but only through a narrower claim and evidence that survives independent reproduction. ([Transfer recalculation, lines 65–124](../research_notes/LANTERN%20research%20readiness%20audit/experiments_reproducibility.md#L65-L124); [NASA/JPL ALHAT](https://robotics.jpl.nasa.gov/what-we-do/research-tasks/alhat-autonomous-landing-and-hazard-avoidance-technology/))

*Audit date: 25 October 2026. This report synthesizes the three completed audit notes, limited direct source fact checks, and essential official-source verification. It recommends repairs; it does not implement them. “Verified” distinguishes source inspection, recalculation of archived data, and actual test execution below. All proposed acceptance gates are project recommendations, not NASA standards.*

## 1. A real closed-loop testbed sits beneath an overextended project claim

### Four execution paths must become four clearly labelled evidence sources

The original objective combines terrain and illumination awareness, camera/altimeter trust, an estimator, guidance, touchdown stability and optional live mapping. The repository implements different parts in different systems. Combining their capabilities into one diagram without execution boundaries would describe a system that has not been demonstrated. ([Original plan and scope audit, lines 24–40](../research_notes/LANTERN%20research%20readiness%20audit/scientific_claims.md#L24-L40))

| Execution path | What actually happens | Defensible evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Original Unity `Site04` demo | `DescentAutopilot.cs` reads true velocity, attitude and terrain height; `LumenModule.cs` independently recovers an injected offset. Starts at rest around 102.5 km AGL. | Flight/scan integration demonstration; matching does not drive that autopilot. |
| Older Python benchmark | `world.py` supplies synthetic terrain/sensors; `engine.py` runs translational dynamics, a nine-state EKF and sensor judges at 0.25 s. Default descent starts at 15 km with substantial horizontal/downward velocity. | Synthetic sensor-trust comparisons, subject to the confirmed defects below. Python “live scan” is a truth-centered noisy position-fix surrogate. |
| New Unity experiments | Editor runner → isolated PhysX scene → ray ranges/acceleration adapter → position/velocity estimate → raw-point matcher → veto/gain → guidance → forces → contact logs, at 0.05 s. | Actual closed-loop horizontal correction experiments under simplified dynamics. No live camera navigation or complete camera/altimeter fault diagnosis. |
| Replays and lander art | CSV interpolation drives displayed vehicles; detailed FBX meshes are illustrative. The saved terrain replay points to geometric v1. | Explanation of recorded cases, not a rerun, current-transfer viewer, optical experiment or validated lander design. |

These distinctions are established by all 27 project C# sources and the Python core review. The new `TerrainNavigationCore.cs` accepts neither a Rigidbody nor a terrain collider; true errors enter evaluation/training labels rather than selected inference features. No direct truth-feature leakage was found in the new matcher/gain interface. This is a meaningful strength, even though the sensor adapter assumes ideal attitude and exact initial velocity. ([Architecture and source boundaries, lines 67–96](../research_notes/LANTERN%20research%20readiness%20audit/implementation_fidelity.md#L67-L96); [core, lines 7–109](../LANTERN/Assets/Scripts/Experiments/TerrainNavigationCore.cs#L7-L109))

### Preserve the engineering strengths and scientific self-correction

The recent work has raw trajectories, frozen portable models, area-grouped training, paired scenario configurations, recorded failures and increasingly explicit protocols. The audit recalculated the principal outcomes, found no cross-split run/site groups in the scan datasets, verified all **1,668 indexed transfer files**, and replayed model predictions on archived inputs to floating-point agreement. The project retained the failed veto and export attempt rather than presenting only a favorable demo. These are foundations for research, although hashes prove artifact identity rather than physical validity or externally witnessed chronology. ([Checks, lines 225–241](../research_notes/LANTERN%20research%20readiness%20audit/experiments_reproducibility.md#L225-L241))

NASA’s SPLICE description confirms the relevance of coupled navigation, sensing, guidance and hazard processing. It distinguishes camera-to-orbital-image TRN, navigation Doppler lidar and hazard-detection lidar; LANTERN’s ray grid should not be described as an implementation of all three. JPL already describes small-lidar-scan registration against a larger topographic map. Adams and colleagues’ **2025 SciTech** paper describes terrain-aware slant-range modeling and PITFAL multibeam consensus. Therefore “we match lidar to a DEM” and “we use terrain to reject bad ranges” are not sufficient contributions. The narrow control-risk study recommended here remains **novelty-unverified**: this audit did not conduct a comprehensive external survey. ([SPLICE](https://www.nasa.gov/safe-and-precise-landing-integrated-capabilities-evolution-splice/); [ALHAT](https://robotics.jpl.nasa.gov/what-we-do/research-tasks/alhat-autonomous-landing-and-hazard-avoidance-technology/); [Adams et al., verified NTRS abstract/record](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240014100))

Scan grouping passed: no run/site groups crossed assigned splits, and utility train/validation had no exactly identical six-feature float32 vectors. Nine Site04 crops had nonoverlapping interiors. Nevertheless, common DEM lineage, repeated noise schedules, selected flat target neighborhoods and prescribed perturbations remain dependencies. The one-crop utility final smoke touched that final crop; transfer improved the design by reserving a separate smoke crop. Site04 had informed earlier guidance/sensor choices and a 100-run pre-batch smoke; Site01 had been used in older tuning, although excluded from this utility model’s fitting/selection. With one final crop and one transfer DEM, population uncertainty is underdetermined. ## 3. Correctness, observability and physical credibility are different blockers

### Fix demonstrated defects before attributing failure to the research idea

**Highest priority for legacy Python claims:** `NavBatch.predict` propagates position with accelerometer bias but omits the corresponding Jacobian block. At DT=0.25, the measured derivative is **−0.03125**, while the code has zero. Delayed altimetry emits uninitialized zero history as measurements. Timestamp compensation predicts past position using velocity but omits velocity terms from the measurement Jacobian. Terminal recording retains pre-contact position, and altitude RMS compares quantities from different timesteps. These are established logic/mathematical defects; their effects on historical aggregate success have not been quantified. ([Reproduced/source findings, lines 106–130](../research_notes/LANTERN%20research%20readiness%20audit/implementation_fidelity.md#L106-L130); [terminal findings, lines 212–220](../research_notes/LANTERN%20research%20readiness%20audit/implementation_fidelity.md#L212-L220))

Legacy map ablations also retain inconsistent information: shifted/missing-map modes still access unshifted high-resolution terrain statistics; unknown off-tile illumination becomes lit when the unknown mask is discarded. In new Unity runners, a failed altimeter ray leaves a stale value that is fused without age/validity. Final navigation error compares the last physical state with an estimate not yet propagated through that interval. Both pipelines can apply a full final thrust step despite insufficient remaining fuel. These require fixes or explicit restrictions; stale-ray and fuel-exhaustion occurrence in reported batches is **unverified**. True target miss and recorded collision speed are not invalidated merely by the separate terminal-estimate timing defect. ([Map interfaces](../sim/engine.py#L215-L232); [fidelity audit, lines 132–138, 172–176, 208–220](../research_notes/LANTERN%20research%20readiness%20audit/implementation_fidelity.md#L132-L220); [illumination, lines 255–264](../research_notes/LANTERN%20research%20readiness%20audit/implementation_fidelity.md#L255-L264))

Secondary confirmed defects include Python trust-export threshold casting at float32/double boundaries, malformed/nonfinite-input handling, zero default test discovery and incomplete verifier completeness checks. Archived classifier inputs nevertheless replay correctly; these boundary defects are not demonstrated causes of transfer harm. Likewise, covariance positive-semidefiniteness loss, harmful matcher-edge behavior and backend collision-velocity inaccuracies are concerns needing tests, not observed failures. ([Reproduction defects R3–R8, lines 249–258](../research_notes/LANTERN%20research%20readiness%20audit/experiments_reproducibility.md#L249-L258))

### A globally shifted map cannot be repaired by a stronger learner seeing the same evidence

Let true terrain height be \(h(q)\), stored map \(m(q)=h(q+s)\), true horizontal vehicle position \(p\), estimate \(\hat p=p+e\), and proposed correction \(c\). Under noiseless, fixed-attitude local shape observations, matching requires

\[
h(p+u_i)=m(\hat p+c+u_i)=h(p+e+c+u_i+s).
\]

For distinctive terrain and adequate search coverage, the shape-consistent solution is

\[
e+c+s=0,\qquad \hat p^+-p=e+c=-s.
\]

Thus an excellent fit can leave absolute target-frame error equal to the map translation. The observation constrains the combined position/map offset, not its decomposition. The actual matcher additionally subtracts mean height residual, removing constant vertical-offset information; over a planar surface, horizontal translation changes height by a constant and becomes unobservable under this cost. Confidence compares the best cost with the mean of distant costs, not a separated second-best minimum, so periodic terrain can remain ambiguous despite high confidence. ([Cost/search, lines 37–80](../LANTERN/Assets/Scripts/Experiments/TerrainNavigationCore.cs#L37-L80); [shift construction and analysis, lines 178–192](../research_notes/LANTERN%20research%20readiness%20audit/implementation_fidelity.md#L178-L192))

An independent constraint is needed **if the claim requires absolute registration to a separately defined target and both position and map shift are otherwise unknown**. Examples are a surveyed landmark/reference or independently registered navigation measurement with stated uncertainty. A camera matching the same displaced reference, another scan of the same terrain, or inertial increments without an absolute prior does not automatically supply that anchor. If the task is only map-relative localization, or an adequate independent position/registration prior already exists, the conclusion changes; model that prior explicitly. A prior can constrain uncertainty without making local geometry itself informative about the missing degree of freedom. More ML can exploit dataset correlations but cannot remove this structural ambiguity in general.

### Scalar correction utility is not estimator covariance or sensor integrity

`NavigationEstimate` integrates position/velocity, overwrites altitude from map-plus-range and nudges horizontal position. It has no bias/attitude state, covariance, measurement cross-correlation or integrity bound. A regression gain is not a Kalman gain, and matcher confidence is not a probability that the map or sensor is healthy. A future uncertainty claim needs a derived measurement distribution over pose, range, map registration and map error; adding terrain variance to \(HPH^T+R\) without checking overlap can double-count position-induced uncertainty. Mean-zero noise and coherent registration error require different treatment. ([Core, lines 86–95](../LANTERN/Assets/Scripts/Experiments/TerrainNavigationCore.cs#L86-L95); [uncertainty derivation gap, lines 192–196](../research_notes/LANTERN%20research%20readiness%20audit/scientific_claims.md#L192-L196))

The proposed three-way diagnosis also overreaches: “altimeter disagrees with map” plus “altimeter disagrees with scan” does not prove map and scan agree, nor exclude multiple faults. Shared pose, timing, terrain and reused range samples create correlated evidence. A complete integrity extension would need explicit map/pose/sensor/multiple-fault hypotheses, all pairwise residuals, an unresolved state, calibrated uncertainty and availability/risk metrics. That extension is not required for the narrower control study, but its absence must constrain the project title and abstract. ([Diagnosis gap, lines 150–154](../research_notes/LANTERN%20research%20readiness%20audit/scientific_claims.md#L150-L154))

### The simulation measures a narrow mechanism, not complete lunar sensor physics

The new lidar is an instantaneous **24×24 ray grid**, ±20° per axis, with a 3 km limit, independent 2% dropout and range noise \(0.05+0.001r\) m. Altimetry is a vertical ray with 0.1 m white noise. Acceleration is differentiated true world velocity plus 0.002 m/s² noise; it is a net-acceleration adapter, not body-frame accelerometer specific force. Attitude is ideal, velocity initially exact, scans arrive every three seconds, and corrections stop at/below measured 80 m AGL. No scan-motion distortion, sensor extrinsics uncertainty, correlated range bias, dust/reflectance return model or causal processing latency is represented. Logged computation time does not move the vehicle while matching. ([Sensor/scheduling implementation, lines 154–170](../research_notes/LANTERN%20research%20readiness%20audit/implementation_fidelity.md#L154-L170))

The terrain has real DEM ancestry, but independent arrays are not independently measured truth. Local truth is sampled at **8 m**, then interpolated to a 513² collider over 2.4 km; original full-tile terrain has approximately **7.8125 m** vertex spacing from nominal 5 m input. Interpolation and approximately 0.071 m height quantization do not create submeter terrain knowledge. Shared-source aligned success risks an overly favorable forward/inverse-model coupling. Test independent resolutions, interpolation, correlated map errors and independently measured/generated fine-scale truth; do not claim resolved footpad/rock hazards from the illustrated lander. Preserve CRS, datum, pixel conventions, nodata, source hashes and transformations; Site01 lineage is stronger than Site04’s. ([DEM construction, lines 194–200](../research_notes/LANTERN%20research%20readiness%20audit/implementation_fidelity.md#L194-L200); [terrain provenance, lines 255–264](../research_notes/LANTERN%20research%20readiness%20audit/implementation_fidelity.md#L255-L264))

Rendered-camera evidence belongs to the older survey: 300 image pairs, same current scene for reference/live views, imposed grayscale noise and ZNCC matching. Python later samples an empirical table rather than rendering during descent, including outside the surveyed altitude band. **Zero >100 m errors among 226 accepted fixes** is not calibrated sub-10 m performance or zero failure risk. Survey-label geometry and rendered-scene geometry also differ. Retain this as renderer-specific evidence; actual camera claims require frozen acquisition settings, independent reference/truth handling, held-out calibration and physically justified exposure/noise assumptions. The new Unity correction experiments use raycasts, not these camera images. ([Camera pipeline, lines 266–274](../research_notes/LANTERN%20research%20readiness%20audit/implementation_fidelity.md#L266-L274); [survey claim limits](../research_notes/LANTERN%20research%20readiness%20audit/scientific_claims.md#L180-L184))

Finally, the new vehicle is a frozen-rotation sphere with instantaneous world-force actuation, not a legged six-degree-of-freedom lander. No tip-over, leg crushing, throttle/gimbal transient or settled stability is measured. That approximation can support a bounded translational-control paper. It cannot justify “upright safe landing,” nor does a 102.5 km drop at rest validate orbital descent. Timestep, fuel and collision-speed checks remain necessary even for the narrow paper. ([Contact/dynamics limitations, lines 202–210](../research_notes/LANTERN%20research%20readiness%20audit/implementation_fidelity.md#L202-L210))

## 4. One focused study can test the position-versus-control tradeoff

### State a falsifiable hypothesis and a competing explanation

**Recommended direction: control-aware scheduling of terrain-relative position corrections under map mismatch.** Proposed hypothesis: within a declared short-descent envelope on reserved terrain, constraining correction gain/rate by estimated remaining control authority and time to contact reduces speed-limit-violating contacts relative to the strongest equally tuned geometric/bounded-gain baseline, without materially reducing target/contact success or worsening the prespecified miss tail. The mechanism predicts that abrupt or poorly timed estimate changes cause commands the vehicle cannot settle before contact. This is a testable candidate contribution, not a verified novel method or promised improvement.

The mechanism has a concrete foothold. Before clipping, the current controller uses \(a_h=-0.04\hat p_h-0.5\hat v_h\); a position update \(\alpha c\) changes acceleration command by \(-0.04\alpha c\), without correcting velocity. Yet the utility label optimizes only

\[
\alpha^*=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\left(-\frac{e^Tc}{c^Tc}\right),
\]

with a special tiny-update exception. That label ignores impact speed, saturation, latency and remaining descent time. Stationary two-scan training also omits policy-induced trajectory history. **Objective mismatch, deployment-history shift and terrain ambiguity are competing explanations**; existing failures do not isolate them. ([Controller](../LANTERN/Assets/Scripts/Experiments/TerrainNavigationCore.cs#L101-L108); [gain target](../sim/scan_utility.py#L5-L12); [training/attribution limits](../research_notes/LANTERN%20research%20readiness%20audit/experiments_reproducibility.md#L19-L42))

Start with a cheap development intervention: replay identical proposed corrections through a verified translational controller at different predeclared application times/gains. Log command jump, saturation duration, velocity response, time-to-contact estimate and final contact. Then perform actual closed-loop trials, where corrections change future observations. A smoothing safeguard could also delay needed braking, so test worsening as well as benefit. Use available estimates and timestamps only; simulation truth is an offline diagnostic/oracle, never an operational policy input.

### Compare against controls capable of winning

| Comparison family | Required role in the same information/control envelope |
|---|---|
| OFF and original geometric correction | Establish no-correction cost and the value of actual matching. |
| Tuned constant gain, magnitude cap and gain/rate scheduling | Test whether ordinary control engineering explains the benefit. Include time-to-contact scheduling without ML. |
| Ambiguity-aware geometric/statistical gate | Use separated minima/local observability and, where a consistent filter exists, innovation uncertainty. Do not call a covariance-free threshold NIS. |
| Existing utility model and safeguarded utility | Isolate the proposed intervention from the already studied local-error objective. |
| Strong applicable published correction/fusion method | Select after a focused full-method literature check; reproduce or document deviations. PITFAL is relevant to multibeam range rejection, not automatically an equivalent baseline for horizontal scan-gain scheduling. |

Every arm receives the same observations, timing, map products, fallback access and controller. Record search spaces, tried configurations, validation calls and actual compute; merely matching threshold counts does not ensure fairness. Tune classical controls seriously: bounded correction already had lower development mean miss than selected hybrid utility, **27.666909 versus 28.179172 m**, though more speed violations. ([Development evidence](../research_notes/LANTERN%20research%20readiness%20audit/experiments_reproducibility.md#L36-L43))

Controlled ablations should remove the safeguard, replace learned gain with the strongest classical schedule, remove temporal/AGL features, compare stationary versus trajectory-based collection, and separate label change from feature/data change. Match training/selection budgets and include a shuffled-label or constant-output control. Apply map shifts as an explicit unobservable negative control; any independent-anchor arm must include anchor-removal and anchor-error tests. Repeat closed-loop evaluation across independently trained seeds—initially three to five if feasible, increasing based on development variability—not just new sensor-noise seeds.

### Freeze an interpretable experiment before accessing new outcomes

Use all existing final/transfer results as development evidence from now on. Reserve new geographic regions with spatial buffers and, where available, independent DEM/acquisition lineage. Split by the highest relevant group before preprocessing. Separate fitting, model selection, calibration, smoke and final test; freeze site-selection criteria without selecting on controller outcomes. Hold out both terrain and defined fault combinations/severities, including correlated map errors, missing/aged ranges and imperfect initial velocity. Record a dated exposure ledger and independently timestamp the protocol/model/config hashes. If only more crops of existing tiles are available, narrow the generalization claim accordingly.

Primary reporting should pair a speed-violation risk difference with success noninferiority and a prespecified miss-tail constraint. Select practical margins and independent-cluster sample size from the declared task and development uncertainty **before testing**; none is an established NASA threshold here. Report contact/noncontact counts, timeout/fuel/map-exit reasons, mean/median/p90/p95/worst-decile miss, impact incidence and severity, per-terrain effects, correction availability, latency/deadline misses and compute. If uncertainty is added, report coverage and consistency; if fault diagnosis is claimed, define reading/event/run denominators and pending/abstention rates.

Pair configurations across arms; bootstrap or model independent terrain clusters with nested runs, and separately quantify fitted-seed variation. Preserve shared random schedules but acknowledge different trajectories produce different measurements. Prespecify one primary comparator and handle other comparisons as secondary or multiplicity-adjusted. Few independent clusters should produce an inconclusive generalization conclusion, not narrow row-bootstrap confidence intervals marketed as site-level evidence.

**Failure/kill gates:** halt performance interpretation if analytic physics/measurement fixtures fail, a hidden truth input appears, required artifacts are missing, or independent registration is falsely inferred from shifted-map fits. Reject the benefit claim if the joint endpoint fails. Reject the learned-component claim if a tuned classical schedule matches it, or if ablations cannot isolate its contribution. Reject a broad terrain claim if effects reverse across independent regions or are too imprecise. If the mechanism hypothesis fails, the fallback is a bounded, reproducible failure-envelope study explaining when local utility is insufficient; that requires correct implementation, strong controls and precise uncertainty, not merely a catalog of failed runs.

## 5. Repair in dependency order, then pursue a research-paper route

### A staged plan should earn evidence before adding scope

### Minimum publishable study and stretch validation are different commitments

The minimum credible study is a corrected, explicitly simplified simulator; observable-information boundaries; analytic/numerical validation; one mechanism hypothesis; strong matched controls; ablations; new grouped holdout; paired cluster-aware estimates and failure tails; and independently reproducible artifacts. It may conclude that a classical schedule wins or that the tested safeguard offers no transferable benefit. Publication still depends on whether that finding advances the verified literature.

Stretch validation adds an independently surveyed terrain rig with real range sensors, motion/extrinsic calibration, causal acquisition latency, a second map source and embedded-hardware profiling. A broader camera-integrity claim additionally requires real image processing and a consistent joint estimator. Flight qualification would demand a much larger vehicle/sensor/environment/software assurance case, including realistic attitude/actuation/contact and hardware-in-the-loop evidence. JPL’s ALHAT validation discussion supports that progression; it does **not** make flight qualification a prerequisite for a bounded research paper. ([ALHAT validation context](https://robotics.jpl.nasa.gov/what-we-do/research-tasks/alhat-autonomous-landing-and-hazard-avoidance-technology/))

**Recommended route:** develop the focused manuscript for an appropriate future **AIAA SciTech GNC** cycle and use its evidence package for technical discussion with relevant researchers. This is an AIAA publication route, not NASA acceptance or endorsement. The verified call includes navigation, sensor fusion, uncertainty and validation; GNC requires a full draft manuscript rather than an abstract. Its currently posted initial deadline was **21 May 2026**; **1 December 2026** is the later manuscript deadline, not a newly available submission window. No future opening was verified. ([Official SciTech call](https://scitech.aiaa.org/call-for-content/call-for-papers/))

A NASA **funding proposal** instead responds to a specific announcement with its technical scope, work plan, team, budget and submission rules. NSPIRES provides announcements and proposal handling; organizational affiliation/registration and SAM requirements apply to that route. Creating an account does not establish eligibility. The user’s nationality, institution and funding arrangement are unknown. NASA Science guidance distinguishes institutional affiliation from citizenship and generally treats non-U.S. institutional research participation as cooperative/no-exchange-of-funds; it cannot be generalized into a guaranteed decision for every NASA solicitation. Confirm the actual announcement with the institution/program contact before preparing an application. ([NSPIRES](https://nspires.nasaprs.com/external/); [NASA SARA FAQ14](https://science.nasa.gov/researchers/sara/faqs/#faq-14))

A flight-integration request is a third activity, requiring program-specific qualification and interface evidence. SPLICE is useful technical context, not an open submission portal; its official milestone list includes project closeout in September2026. No matching open NASA solicitation, numeric mission requirement or eligibility determination was established here. Do not send the unreconciled historical PDFs as proof of a completed system. ## Conclusion

The useful research opportunity is to explain and bound a failure that the project already exposes: a correction can improve position while degrading the controlled terminal outcome. Turning that observation into knowledge requires isolating the mechanism and showing when a practical intervention works, fails or is unnecessary. A negative answer with strong controls is more defensible than a broad lunar-safety claim supported by favorable means.

The decisive next milestone is a small, corrected experiment that an independent reviewer can reproduce and falsify. Once it survives, the project has a credible basis for a focused aerospace manuscript and an informed NASA-facing conversation; until then, additional models, graphics or correlated simulation runs do not resolve the central evidence gaps.
