## 1. What was proposed, what was actually studied, and which claims survived?

### Takeaway

The documentary record supports a progression from a broad terrain/lighting-informed sensor-trust proposal to separate numerical filtering studies and, later, a narrower estimated-state Unity scan-correction study. The latest frozen-model transfer **failed its own combined error/speed criterion**; a reliable joint camera/altimeter integrity system, method novelty, and physical mission readiness remain unestablished. This is a basis for a focused controlled failure-analysis study, not evidence that LANTERN has solved lunar sensor trust. ### Inferences

- The most defensible current question is already close to the overview's formulation: when to apply/reduce/reject a terrain correction, judged by full landing outcomes. Narrow it further to the observed discrepancy between local position utility and contact-speed risk. The evidence justifies investigating this failure; it does not prove a novel general principle. [Overview:24–39](../../docs/PROJECT_EXPLAINED_AND_REFOCUSED.md#L24-L39); [transfer:49–66](../../docs/SCAN_UTILITY_TRANSFER_RESULTS.md#L49-L66).
- The record contains substantive self-correction rather than a monotonic success story: camera confound, timestamp confound, failed learned veto, narrow utility recovery, failed transfer, then narrower novelty framing. These failures belong in the main scientific narrative, not a hidden appendix. [FULL_LOG:85–179,330–527](../../docs/FULL_LOG.md#L85-L527).
- Later experimental protocols are more careful about prior exposure and correlated observations than the older PDFs. That improvement is real at the documentation level, but does not make earlier claims valid retroactively. [Preparation:75–81](../../docs/SCAN_TRUST_ML_NEXT_EXPERIMENT.md#L75-L81); [transfer protocol:7–18,57–63](../../docs/SCAN_UTILITY_TRANSFER_PROTOCOL.md#L7-L63).

### Gaps

- Whether any runtime actually implements the promised full sensor-integrity loop, whether truth leaks into inference/guidance, and whether metric/routing verifiers are correct are questions for the code/raw-data researchers; document statements alone do not settle them.
- Whether the mission explanations, external novelty comparisons, publication metadata and conference dates are accurate is reserved for the external-literature researcher. This audit verifies only local wording and internal consistency.
- Whether there is an unpublished narrower contribution cannot be established. No endorsement of unpublished novelty is made.

## 2. Which deficiencies block a credible research submission, and what would resolve them?

### Takeaway

The principal blockers are inferential, not cosmetic: an undefined submission claim, contaminated “unseen” framing, incompletely matched comparisons, no isolated explanation of utility transfer failure, and unvalidated physical/sensor assumptions. Correcting documentation alone can make the record honest; claiming improved reliability requires additional, prospectively bounded evidence. [Exposure](../../docs/PREREG.md#L23-L24); [matched-ablation gap](../../docs/SCAN_UTILITY_RESULTS.md#L166-L170); [failed transfer](../../docs/SCAN_UTILITY_TRANSFER_RESULTS.md#L7-L19).

### Cited Findings

Severity: **Critical** blocks the corresponding scientific claim/submission framing; **High** threatens validity, reproducibility or interpretation; **Medium** is a significant documentation/communication defect. Priorities P0/P1/P2 indicate order, not an assertion of an official NASA process. Every gate below is a proposed project acceptance rule.

#### F01 — Critical / P0: No single completed contribution matches the broad project pitch
- **Evidence (DOC):** README title/opening is sensor trust for lunar south-pole landing; current novelty review explicitly says Unity scales scan position corrections and does not implement complete joint camera/altimeter diagnosis. [README:1–5](../../README.md#L1-L5); [review:138–151](../../docs/RESEARCH_NOVELTY_REVIEW.md#L138-L151). Older plan describes an EKF plus camera, altimeter, scan mixing and source diagnosis. `4_Proposed_Methodology.pdf`, pp. 1–5, 10–12. [PDF](../../deliverables/4_Proposed_Methodology.pdf).
- **Consequence (INFERENCE):** A reviewer cannot tell whether the proposed contribution is predictive gating, lidar localization, learned utility, complete sensor integrity, or map compression. Evidence from separate systems is liable to be combined into a capability none actually demonstrates.
- **Remedy:** Define one manuscript contribution: a controlled characterization of when correction-selection objectives improve localization yet harm landing control under map mismatch. Keep the broader integrity system as future work unless it is separately implemented and evaluated.
- **Acceptance gate:** A one-paragraph contribution statement, one system diagram and a claim-to-experiment table identify the exact implementation, supported outcomes and exclusions. Every abstract assertion maps to a completed experiment or is labelled hypothesis/future work. A reviewer can identify the one falsifiable question without reading project history.

Competing hypotheses should distinguish (a) local position-optimal gain versus controllability/time-to-contact, (b) stationary-training/deployment-history shift, and (c) terrain/map ambiguity. A cap-only/classical-gain arm is essential. The record presently cannot choose among these causes. [Utility target](../../docs/SCAN_TRUST_PROTOCOL_V2.md#L44-L52); [component confound](../../docs/SCAN_UTILITY_RESULTS.md#L166-L170); [transfer failure](../../docs/SCAN_UTILITY_TRANSFER_RESULTS.md#L49-L66).
3. **P1 — New confirmatory evaluation.** Freeze the chosen explanation/intervention, equal-budget controls, exact endpoints and cluster-aware analysis before new area outcomes. Failure must remain an acceptable deliverable. Do not promise a desired result or select a new primary metric afterwards.
4. **P1/P2 — External validity matched to claim.** A simulation-only paper does not inherently require flight certification or an orbital start, but does require a credible, bounded simulator and the appropriate validation evidence for its claims. A mission-readiness claim would require a much stronger physical/operational case than any current document supplies. [Overview:105–109,175–193](../../docs/PROJECT_EXPLAINED_AND_REFOCUSED.md#L105-L193).

Suggested **candidate**, not verified-novel, falsifiable question:

> Under reserved terrain and map mismatch, does an uncertainty- and contact-risk-aware correction policy reduce unsafe contact outcomes without sacrificing target success compared with equally tuned geometric and bounded-gain controls, and do controlled ablations show whether any benefit arises from the learned gain or the safeguard?

If the intervention does not improve outcomes, the study can still establish a bounded failure envelope if implementation correctness, sensitivity, fair controls and uncertainty are demonstrated. It should not claim “ML cannot work” or “local utility always harms safety.” The existing record motivates this question but has not answered it. [Current question](../../docs/PROJECT_EXPLAINED_AND_REFOCUSED.md#L24-L39); [future requirements](../../docs/SCAN_UTILITY_TRANSFER_RESULTS.md#L102-L107).

### Gaps

- No numeric new margin, sample size or NASA operational limit is prescribed here because the claimed operating envelope and receiving program are not established. Those values must come from a named task/mission and development/power analysis, not be chosen after test outcomes.
- No claim is made that adding a safeguard will improve success, that a new uncertainty formulation will be novel, or that the proposed failure study will be accepted.
- The code/data owners should prioritize independent checks of endpoint denominators, truth isolation, crop ancestry/footprints, common random number routing, v1/v2 source identity, raw/partial-update labels, and reproducibility.
